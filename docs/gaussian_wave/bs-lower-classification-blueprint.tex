\begin{abstract}
This is the updated blueprint for the lower and qualitative classification
chain, first written before its implementation. The process-level chain is
now implemented: actual uniform simple-regular-graph counting, all-radius
randomized recoloring, differential entropy, Gaussian reference asymptotics,
strong-subadditivity equality and full-process propagation. Smoothness is
removed by adding one independent Gaussian wave, whitening its strictly
positive root covariance, classifying that sum, and cancelling the Gaussian
in characteristic functions. No conservation of source variance, entropy
inequality, Gaussian classification or smoothing-typicality assertion is a
hypothesis of the assembled process theorem. The public spectral and random-graph interfaces are built. The final
whole-project verification record is reported separately from this blueprint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and the actual process-level theorem}
Fix $d=m+2\geq3$, finitely many channels $r\geq0$, and a common energy
$|\lambda|<2\sqrt{d-1}$. Let $G_n$ be labeled simple $d$-regular graphs on
$N_n$ vertices, with $N_n\to\infty$. Mark their vertices by vectors
$f_n(v)\in\R^r$ and assume only a uniform empirical second-moment bound
\[
 \frac1{N_n}\sum_v|f_n(v)|^2\leq S.
\]
Sampling uses an actual uniform vertex and uniformly ordered finite graph
covers of the explicit word tree. Write $P$ for a whole-tree probability
law whose finite cover windows are weak limits of these marked graphs.

\begin{theorem}[Implemented common-good-graph classification]
There are graph events $\mathcal H_n$ whose probabilities tend to one under
the actual uniform labeled simple $d$-regular-graph law. Suppose
$G_n\in\mathcal H_n$ eventually. If $P$ is invariant under all tree
automorphisms, has finite coordinate second moments, and satisfies
\[
 \sum_{w\sim v}X_w=\lambda X_v
 \quad\text{almost surely for every channel and every }v,
\]
then every finite joint window of $P$ is centered Gaussian.
\end{theorem}

The public process endpoint is
\lean{common_graph_eigenprocess_finite_gaussian} in
\lean{BS/CommonGraphEigenprocessClassification.lean}. Its source inputs
are the actual graph marks, their cover-window weak limit, the second-moment
bound, invariance and the common-energy eigenrelation. The finite recoloring
lower bound, entropy witnesses, Gaussianity of the smoothed process and
Gaussian cancellation are all derived internally. The proved ordinary spectral
adapter derives the limit assumptions from bounded empirical $L^2$ and
vanishing empirical $L^2$ residuals; its public endpoints are stated below.

Let $C=\E[X_oX_o^{\mathsf T}]\geq0$. The eigenrelation and invariance imply
zero means since $|\lambda|<d$, so $C=\Cov(X_o)$. Weak convergence may lose
second moments, rank, or all variance. The qualitative Lean conclusion is
\lean{CenteredGaussianLaw}, with the covariance determined by the actual
limit law. It does not assert preservation of the graph normalization.
The existing fourth-moment/uniform-integrability layer is needed when the
bulk application requires a prescribed variance-one covariance. The joint
proof treats all channels together at the same energy; it does not infer
independence from separate Gaussian marginals or zero covariances.

\section{L0--L1: geometry, counting, and one common graph event}
Put $C_0=B_1(o)$, let $e_0$ be one distinguished edge, and set
$C_k=B_k(C_0)$ and $e_k=B_k(e_0)$. Let $W_\lambda(S)$ be the literal
solution space of the eigenrelations at the interior vertices of the finite
tree $S$. The finite charts and all sampling maps are actual linear maps.
They satisfy
\[
 \dim W_\lambda(C_k)=d(d-1)^k,\qquad
 \dim W_\lambda(e_k)=2(d-1)^k,\qquad
 |C_k|-\frac d2|e_k|=1.
\]
Thus the intrinsic dimension balance is zero while the ambient cardinality
balance is one, multiplied by $r$ for the joint process. The implementation
constructs the infinite word tree, its finite balls, automorphisms,
rerootings, local covers, path lifting, and the complete center-fixing
finite-ball stabilizers. These are proved geometry, not adapter premises.

For any finite alphabet $F$ and continuous probability-mass kernel
$\phi:\R^r\to\mathcal P(F)$, recolor vertices independently conditional on
the marks. Direct finite counting, profile concentration, and weak limits
give for every $k$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite}
 H((\phi_*X)_{C_k})-\frac d2H((\phi_*X)_{e_k})
 \geq \int H(\phi(x))\,\dd\Law(X_o)(x).
\end{equation}
The conditional recoloring entropy on the right is retained. The kernel
is arbitrary and ordinary; no union bound over real-valued kernels is used.
A summable palette slack gives one event for every finite alphabet. Actual
finite product coloring laws and dependency-variance estimates control the
local observations, and finite path-word codes implement higher-radius
recoding.

Independent finite markers make the center-fixing group actions free with
probability tending to one. Recovery is proved on the good marker set;
a quantitative error controls the complement. Exact orbit-fiber entropy
and the actual stabilizer orders cancel the group terms. Marker entropy
then cancels with the right side of \eqref{eq:finite}. Radius and marker
alphabet are fixed before the graph limit. The endpoint is
\lean{common_graph_all_radius_finite_recoloring_entropy_lower}
in \lean{Lower/AllRadiusEntropyLower.lean}; the marker cancellation theorem
is \lean{ball_path_codes_marker_entropy_cancellation}.

For local tree-likeness, the proof uses direct switchings in the simple
ensemble. It bounds prescribed edges and injective short cycles. A collision
of a locally injective cover implies an actual nearby short cycle, using
nonbacktracking paths and cycle extraction. Consequently the fraction of
roots with a colliding depth-$k$ cover tends to zero in probability for every
fixed $k$. A proved slow diagonal produces $K_n\to\infty$ and the event
\lean{CommonGraphGood}: all palettes are good and the bad-root fraction is
less than $1/(K_n+1)$ for every $k\leq K_n$.

Precisely, \lean{exists_common_graph_good_events} in
\lean{Lower/CommonGraphEvents.lean} proves
\[
 \Pr\{G_n\notin\mathcal H_n\}\longrightarrow0
\]
for every growing admissible size sequence with nonempty simple-regular
ensembles and $N_nd=2E_n$. There is no local-tree or graph-counting premise
in this probability theorem. Its palette failure is bounded eventually by
$\exp(-\sqrt{N_n})$; the moving-radius part uses the slow diagonal rather
than an asserted uniform rate.

\section{L2--L3: finite alphabets to intrinsic entropy}
For an arbitrary finite-second-moment law, let $f_t$ be its actual positive
heat density. Posterior Jensen/log-sum and Fubini prove the translation bound
\[
 D(f_t(x)\,\dd x\,\|\,f_t(x+h)\,\dd x)
 \leq\frac{|h|^2}{4t}.
\]
The theorem is \lean{heatDensity_translation_kl}. The finite-output kernel
\lean{heatQuantizerMass} is continuous in the original color, normalized,
and is the actual law of a quantized Gaussian translate. Its block law is
proved to be the product recoloring law. First remove finite clipping at a
fixed grid mesh, then refine the mesh. These limits, including their root
conditional entropy, prove
\[
 h(X_{C_k}+\sigma Z)-\frac d2h(X_{e_k}+\sigma Z)
 \geq r\,h(N(0,\sigma^2))
\]
for independent ambient noise at every vertex. The finite-to-differential
endpoint is \lean{heat_entropy_lower_of_finite_recoloring}.

An actual orthogonal decomposition of each finite window into
$W_\lambda(S)^r$ and its normal space factors the heat density and its
entropy. The normal Gaussian is independent of both the signal and the
intrinsic Gaussian noise. L0's dimension identities cancel exactly the
root noise term. Heat-entropy recovery as $t\downarrow0$, with finite second
moments and absolutely integrable density times log density, gives
\[
 h_{\rm intr}(X_{C_k})-\frac d2h_{\rm intr}(X_{e_k})\geq0.
\]
The actual process theorem is \lean{process_intrinsic_entropy_lower}; its
finite-dimensional helper is
\lean{intrinsic_entropy_nonneg_of_finite_recoloring}.

This ambient heat noise serves the entropy approximation only. The
independent Gaussian \emph{wave} added in L8 below is a different actual
process and obeys the eigenrelation. No entropy in a singular ambient
space is used in the argument.

\section{L4--L7: relative entropy, equality, and all finite balls}
Let $G$ be the actual variance-one Gaussian wave, with independent channels.
The finite covariance calculation and its nonzero spectrum prove
\[
 g_k:=h_{\rm intr}(G_{C_k})-\frac d2h_{\rm intr}(G_{e_k})\longrightarrow0
\]
in the strict bulk. Repeated descendant blocks cancel; the remaining
positive factors have the same linear leading behavior. This is the proved
covariance/pseudodeterminant calculation
\lean{joint_gaussian_ball_reference_entropy_tendsto_zero}, not a reference
asymptotic supplied as a theorem parameter.

For actual positive finite-entropy density laws, strong subadditivity and
its equality case are proved by constructing the conditional-independent
gluing over the shared marginal. Literal sibling-difference innovation
coordinates identify the C/E overlaps and cores. Apply the inequalities in
the same invertible chart to the unknown law and its Gaussian reference.
Their Jacobian terms cancel in relative entropy; no determinant-one claim
is needed. The actual process results are
\lean{process_vertex_relative_ssa_nonneg} and
\lean{process_edge_relative_ssa_nonneg}; their finite-ball equality theorems
are \lean{vertexBall_relative_ssa_equality_iff} and
\lean{edgeBall_relative_ssa_equality_iff}.

Write $\alpha(S)=h_{\rm intr}(X_S)-h_{\rm intr}(G_S)$ and
$b_k=\alpha(C_k)-(d/2)\alpha(e_k)$. The overlap inequalities prove that
$b_k$ is nonincreasing. The intrinsic lower bound gives $b_k\geq-g_k$;
since $g_k\to0$, every $b_k\geq0$. These conclusions are
\lean{process_relative_entropy_balance_antitone} and
\lean{process_relative_entropy_balance_nonneg}.

The jointly colored heat-flow upper theorem gives $b_0\leq0$ for an actual
invariant eigenprocess with identity root second moments. Its equality
case identifies the entire joint star law; the raw-coordinate bridge is
\lean{process_star_relative_upper_and_gaussian_equality}. Thus all $b_k$
and both nonnegative overlap gaps vanish. Strong-subadditivity equality
then propagates the Gaussian base law recursively through every finite
ball. This is \lean{all_ball_laws_eq_gaussian_of_zero_gaps}, followed by
\lean{process_eq_jointGaussian_of_zero_gaps}. Equality on exhausting joint
balls determines the whole countable-process law.

The resulting smooth theorem is
\lean{smooth_normalized_eigenprocess_eq_jointGaussian} in
\lean{BS/SmoothEigenprocessClassification.lean}. Its intermediate inputs
are genuine invariance, coordinate $L^2$, the common eigenrelation, identity
root second moments, the all-radius finite recoloring property, and positive
finite-entropy witnesses for the actual raw C/E innovation laws. The next
section constructs every extra input when applying it to an arbitrary
source process. Neither a Gaussian star nor grafting independence is
assumed.

\section{L8: add one Gaussian wave, whiten, classify, cancel}
Let the source be $X\sim P$ and let $G$ be an independent variance-one
$r$-channel Gaussian wave of the same energy. Put
\[
 C=\E[X_oX_o^{\mathsf T}],\qquad
 A=(C+I)^{1/2},\qquad B=A^{-1},\qquad Y=B(X+G).
\]
The source matrix $C$ may be singular or zero. Since $C+I$ is positive
definite, $B$ is invertible on all $r$ channels. This construction avoids
assuming an entropy density on a deficient support or assuming that the
source covariance is conserved from finite graphs.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Actual graph realization of the independent sum.}
Strict-bulk Gaussian waves are weak limits of concrete finite-radius
Gaussian block factors. Local iid Gaussian sampling, exact no-collision
window laws, covariance matching and dependency concentration construct
marks on a subsequence of the same graphs. Their entire lifted law tends
to $P*G$, with a uniform empirical second-moment bound. The theorem is
\lean{exists_graph_realization_independent_gaussian_wave_sum}. This proves
smoothing closure rather than taking it as an input.
\item \textbf{Finite recoloring lower bound after whitening.}
L1 applied to those realizations gives
\lean{common_graph_gaussian_wave_sum_finite_recoloring_entropy_lower}.
Composing any continuous finite-output kernel with $B$ proves
\lean{finite_recoloring_entropy_lower_channelProcessMap}; hence $Y$ has
all the lower-bound inputs needed by the smooth theorem.
\item \textbf{Ordinary moments, invariance and eigenrelations.}\par
The actual law \lean{whitenedGaussianSumLaw} is defined from a literal
product probability measure and pushforwards. Invariance and common-energy
eigenrelations are preserved. The Gaussian reference has proved zero means,
identity root second moments and finite coordinate second moments. Thus
$X_o+G_o$ has actual second-moment matrix $C+I$, and $Y_o$ has identity
second moments. These are the theorems in
\lean{BS/WhitenedGaussianSum.lean} and
\lean{BS/JointGaussianProcessProperties.lean}.
\item \textbf{Actual positive finite-entropy witnesses.}
Each raw finite-ball Gaussian innovation factor is surjective. An arbitrary
finite-second-moment law convolved with that Gaussian has a positive density
with absolutely integrable density times log density. Innovation extraction
commutes with independent addition and with the same invertible channel
map at every site. Consequently
\lean{whitenedGaussianSumVertexInnovationDensity} and
\lean{whitenedGaussianSumEdgeInnovationDensity}, with their exact
\lean{_law} theorems, supply all raw C/E witnesses. No density of $P$ is
assumed, including when $C$ is singular. Applying L4--L7 proves
\lean{common_graph_whitened_eigenprocess_classification}:
$\Law(Y)=\Law(G)$ as actual whole-process probability laws.
\item \textbf{Recover the source without a small-noise limit.}
Invert the whitening to obtain $\Law(X+G)=\Law(AG)$. On every finite joint
window, independence gives a product of characteristic functions. The
Gaussian factor is nonzero and can be cancelled. Actual second-moment
addition identifies the remaining covariance with the source's actual
second-moment matrix; its positive semidefiniteness is proved before the
Gaussian conclusion. The finite-window theorem is
\lean{finiteGaussian_of_classified_whitened_sum}, using
\lean{centeredGaussian_of_independent_sum}. This gives the qualitative
source theorem stated at the start. One fixed independent Gaussian wave
suffices; no $a\downarrow0$ step, fourth moment or variance-preservation
premise is used.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Verification status, public interfaces, and scope}
The complete standalone Lean 4.19 \lean{scripts/check.sh} check passed:

axioms, 7,082 build targets completed, and the logical dependencies of
5,509 imported \lean{GaussianWave} theorems were audited, including private
declarations. All 396 BS modules, including the public random-graph and

\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, and \lean{Quot.sound}.
The independent semantic review found no hidden Gaussianity, entropy-bound,
or covariance-conservation premise in the assembled classification chain.

The public graph-limit, random-graph-event and spectral adapter modules
now build. The graph-limit endpoints
are \lean{common_graph_approximate_window_limit_gaussian} and
\lean{common_graph_approximate_BSJointClassification}. Their ordinary
inputs are bounded empirical root $L^2$ and empirical $L^2$ residuals tending
to zero, with each channel energy tending to the same strict-bulk energy.
They derive whole-process tightness, actual invariant eigenprocess limits,
and classification of every weak subsequential finite-window limit.
The actual event API is \lean{exists_RRGClassificationEvents}, with
\lean{RRGClassificationEvents.good_probability_tendsto_one} and
\lean{RRGClassificationEvents.approximate_BSJointClassification}.

The literal spectral star sampler is identified with the lifted $C_0$
window; its empirical moments and matrix residuals are proved equal to the
graph quantities. The public selected-eigenvector theorem is
\lean{RRGClassificationEvents.eigenvectors_BSJointClassification}.
It permits arbitrary graph-dependent choices, including repeated channels,
whose eigenvalues tend to one common strict-bulk energy. The theorem
\lean{RRGClassificationEvents.signals_BSJointClassification} also permits
arbitrary bounded-$L^2$ signals with vanishing actual matrix residuals.
The corresponding non-event helpers are
\lean{common_graph_eigenvectors_BSJointClassification} and
\lean{common_graph_signal_BSJointClassification}. None requires Gaussianity,
entropy, a fourth moment, or covariance preservation as an input.

These are qualitative conclusions: every weak subsequential joint star
law is centered Gaussian. Root second-moment bounds establish actual
precompactness, so the limit classification is not vacuous. Prescribed
normalized covariance and Wasserstein convergence belong to the separate
uniform-integrability layer. The final whole-project build and recursive
source/axiom audit are recorded in the project's \lean{audit.txt}; this
blueprint does not replace that verification record or assert that the
separate quantitative inputs are proved.

The counting dependency closure was copied, with attribution, from the
user-authorized sibling \emph{GeneralizedBulk} development into
\lean{GaussianWave.BS.Reused}. It is rebuilt locally and does not import
an external machine path. The manuscript-editing chat
\emph{JH Edit d-regular graph bulk universality article} supplies downstream
mathematical context, not Lean proof evidence. Conditional sibling results
with HY or BS input fields were not substituted for this classification.
