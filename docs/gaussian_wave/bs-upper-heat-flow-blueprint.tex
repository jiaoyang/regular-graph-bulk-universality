\begin{abstract}
This stage concerns only the upper bound in Backhausz--Szegedy, Section 11:
the Gaussian star maximizes the intrinsic star-minus-edge differential entropy.
The lower entropy bound, typical-process classification, and random-graph
transfer are outside this stage. The main generalization is to finitely many
channels at one common eigenvalue, allowing a singular root covariance by
first restricting to its support. No Gaussian-classification premise or
entropy-upper-bound premise is permitted in the final Lean theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Precise target and scope}
Fix $d\geq3$, an integer $r\geq1$, and a common energy
$|\lambda|<2\sqrt{d-1}$. Write
\[
 H_\lambda=\{x\in\R^{d+1}:\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^d x_i=\lambda x_0\},
 \qquad H=H_\lambda^r.
\]
Let $P$ be a probability law on $H$, with the following hypotheses only:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Its mean is zero and its covariance is $I_r\otimes\Sigma_\lambda$,
where $\Sigma_\lambda$ has diagonal entries one, root--leaf entries
$\lambda/d$, and distinct-leaf entries $(\lambda^2-d)/(d(d-1))$.
\item Its law is unchanged by simultaneous neighbor permutations in all
channels. Each root--one-neighbor marginal is unchanged by simultaneous
exchange of its root and neighbor channel vectors.
\item Its intrinsic star entropy and its $2r$-dimensional edge entropy
are finite. All integrability claims implicit in these entropies must be
represented explicitly in Lean; a totalized integral is not a substitute.
\end{enumerate}
Let $\Gamma$ be the actual product of $r$ independent variance-one
Gaussian stars at $\lambda$. Put
\[
 \F(P)=h_H(P)-\frac d2h(P_e).
\]
Here $h_H$ uses induced Euclidean volume on $H$. This is not ambient
differential entropy on $\R^{r(d+1)}$.

\begin{theorem}[Upper bound, with strictness in the bulk]
Under these hypotheses, $\F(P)\leq\F(\Gamma)$.
Equality holds if and only if $P=\Gamma$.
\end{theorem}

The inequality also has a closed-spectral-interval version: the frame
weights remain nonnegative at $|\lambda|=2\sqrt{d-1}$. Strictness there
requires an additional one-zero-weight argument; it is a distinct
milestone, not included silently in the strict-bulk equality proof.
The strict-bulk theorem is the version needed on the compact bulk
interval of the universality manuscript.

For a general root covariance $C\geq0$, restrict every channel vector
to $\operatorname{ran}C$, whiten there, apply the theorem with
$r=\operatorname{rank}C$, and transform back. Zero-variance channels
vanish almost surely at every fixed site. Their removal precedes entropy
calculations. This yields a jointly Gaussian star with covariance
$C\otimes\Sigma_\lambda$, rather than asserting independence before
whitening. The scalar theorem is $r=1$ and will be implemented first.

\section{Whitened geometry and the functional}
Let $U=\{z\in\R^d:\sum_i z_i=0\}$ and
$E=(\R\oplus U)^r$, with its actual Euclidean structure. A fixed invertible
linear map $L:E\to H$ sends the standard Gaussian law to $\Gamma$.
For $Y=L^{-1}X$, covariance is the identity. Each physical edge is an
invertible linear image of the orthogonal projection onto
\[
 V_i=\bigoplus_{a=1}^r\operatorname{span}(w_a,e_{i,a})\subset E,
 \qquad \dim V_i=2r.
\]
The $e_i$ are unit regular-simplex vectors in $U$ with
$\langle e_i,e_j\rangle=-1/(d-1)$ for $i\ne j$.
Change-of-variable determinants cancel upon subtracting the Gaussian
benchmark. Consequently the desired upper bound is equivalent to
\[
 h(Y)-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^d h(P_iY)
 \leq h(G)-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^d h(P_iG),
 \qquad G\sim N(0,I_E).
\]
Since $\dim E=dr$ and $\frac12\sum_i\dim V_i=dr$, this functional is
unchanged by scalar dilation. This dimension cancellation is essential.

\section{The proof in seven steps}
\paragraph{U1. Construct the heat flow.}
For an arbitrary probability law $\mu$ on $E$, independently sample
$Y\sim\mu$ and $G\sim N(0,I_E)$ and set
\[
 Y_t=Y+\sqrt{2t}G,\qquad
 f_t(x)=\int (4\pi t)^{-dr/2}
               \exp[-|x-y|^2/(4t)]\,\dd\mu(y),\quad t>0.
\]
Prove that this is the density of the actual convolution law, is strictly
positive and smooth, and solves $\partial_tf_t=\Delta f_t$.
The statement must cover arbitrary mixing measures. A proof only for
finite Gaussian mixtures does not establish this target.

\paragraph{U2. Prove de Bruijn and score projection.}
For each $t>0$, establish the required integrability, differentiation
under the integral, and integration by parts, giving
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}h(Y_t)=\I(f_t),\qquad
 \I(f)=\int|\nabla\log f|^2f.
\]
The same statements apply to every projected flow $P_iY_t$.
If $g_{i,t}$ is its density, prove the score identity
\[
 \nabla\log g_{i,t}(P_ix)
   =\E[P_i\nabla\log f_t(Y_t)\mid P_iY_t=P_ix].
\]
One can implement this first as an explicit fiber-integral identity and
then identify conditional expectation. This avoids placing the desired
Fisher inequality in a hypothesis structure.

\paragraph{U3. Prove the weighted-frame Fisher inequality.}
Set $c=\lambda/d$, $\rho=\sqrt{1-c^2}$, and
\[
 \alpha=\frac{\sqrt{1+c}+\sqrt{1-c}}2,\qquad
 \beta=\frac{\sqrt{1+c}-\sqrt{1-c}}2,
 \quad A_{i,a}=\alpha w_a-\beta e_{i,a},\quad
 B_{i,a}=\beta w_a+\alpha e_{i,a}.
\]
These pairs are orthonormal in $V_i$, and edge reversal exchanges $A_{i,a}$
with $B_{i,a}$. Define
\[
 t_A=\frac{\rho-1+2/d}{2\rho},\qquad
 t_B=\frac{\rho+1-2/d}{2\rho}.
\]
Prove $t_A,t_B>0$, $t_A+t_B=1$, and the literal frame identity
\[
 \sum_{i,a}(t_A A_{i,a}A_{i,a}^T+t_B B_{i,a}B_{i,a}^T)=I_E.
\]
Conditional-expectation contraction and reversal yield an exact sum of
nonnegative conditional-score variances:
\[
 \I(f_t)-\frac12\sum_i\I(g_{i,t})\geq0.
\]
The weights and the score identities, not Gaussianity of $Y_t$, justify
this inequality. Both Gaussian noise and the orthogonal symmetries preserve
the admissibility conditions along the heat flow.

\paragraph{U4. Differentiate the entropy functional.}
For
$\Phi(t)=h(Y_t)-\frac12\sum_i h(P_iY_t)$, U2--U3 give
\[
 \Phi'(t)=\I(f_t)-\frac12\sum_i\I(g_{i,t})\geq0.
\]
Prove monotonicity on every compact time interval contained in $(0,\infty)$.
No lower entropy bound or graph-counting input occurs here.

\paragraph{U5. Identify the large-time endpoint.}
By dilation invariance,
$\Phi(t)=\Phi(\Law((Y+\sqrt{2t}G)/\sqrt{1+2t}))$.
With $\Cov Y=I$, ordinary Gaussian maximum entropy gives the upper entropy
bound. Entropy concavity of arbitrary Gaussian mixtures gives
\[
 h(G)+\frac{dr}{2}\log\frac{2t}{1+2t}
 \leq h((Y+\sqrt{2t}G)/\sqrt{1+2t})\leq h(G).
\]
Analogous bounds hold in each $V_i$. Squeezing each entropy proves
$\Phi(t)\to\Phi(G)$, so $\Phi(t)\leq\Phi(G)$ for every $t>0$.
Weak convergence alone would not justify passage of differential entropy.

\paragraph{U6. Recover the original law.}
Prove $h(Y_t)\to h(Y)$ and $h(P_iY_t)\to h(P_iY)$ as $t\downarrow0$
under the stated finite-second-moment and finite-entropy hypotheses.
One route is entropy monotonicity under convolution plus lower
semicontinuity of relative entropy after normalization. The corresponding
analytic facts must be proved, not supplied as the final upper-bound input.
U4--U6 imply $\F(P)\leq\F(\Gamma)$.

\paragraph{U7. Prove equality only for the Gaussian.}
If the original functional equals its Gaussian endpoint, monotonicity
forces the Fisher defect to vanish at every positive time. Since both
weights are positive, every directional score in $V_i$ equals its
conditional projection. Positivity of $f_t$ upgrades almost-everywhere
identities to smooth identities. Differentiation gives
\[
 D^3\log f_t(x)[(I-P_i)u,P_iv,w]=0.
\]
The mixed-derivative kernel and neighbor symmetry force
\[
 \log f_t(x)=h_t(x_{\mathrm{root}})
       +\text{a constant channel quadratic form in the transverse coordinates}.
\]
Thus root and transverse vectors are independent and the transverse vector
is Gaussian. The prescribed covariance fixes its covariance.
After normalization, root--leaf reversal says the actual root law is
stationary under
\[
 R\longmapsto cR+\sqrt{1-c^2}\,Z,\qquad
 Z\sim N(0,I_r)\text{ independent of }R.
\]
Prove uniqueness of this stationary law by iterating its characteristic
function: the remaining factor is evaluated at $c^nt\to0$. Hence the root
is Gaussian, so the whole heated star is Gaussian. Finally cancel the
nonzero Gaussian characteristic factor to recover Gaussianity of the
original $Y$. This proves strictness without assuming a classification
theorem. The local upper bound alone does not propagate to all tree sites;
the 2-Markov propagation belongs to the later lower-bound/equality stage.

\section{Source correspondence and verification status}
The source is Backhausz--Szegedy, \emph{On the almost eigenvectors of random
regular graphs}, Ann. Probab. 47 (2019), 1677--1725, supplied PDF:
Section 11, Theorem 11.2, Proposition 11.1, and Lemmas 11.3--11.4;
Appendix A supplies heated-density analytic estimates. The blueprint
generalizes the upper-bound argument to common-energy channel vectors.
The upper-bound package now proves the analytic and geometric steps U1--U7
for the stated strict-bulk, common-energy version. Its final scalar and joint
theorems do not assume Gaussianity, a Fisher inequality, de Bruijn's identity,
entropy convergence, or independence. The hypotheses record actual laws,
second moments, source symmetries, and finite entropy where the unheated
functional is used. Unit second moments themselves imply genuine second
integrability. For singular channel covariance, zero directions are removed
using proved almost-everywhere and actual-law reconstruction before any
entropy is evaluated.

The project uses Lean 4.19.0 and pinned Mathlib revision
\begin{center}\small\texttt{c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b}.
\end{center}
\texttt{scripts/check.sh} runs a recursive source audit, the Lake build, and
a logical-dependency audit. The source check rejects admitted proofs and
source-declared axioms. Every audited theorem may use only
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound};
\texttt{audit.txt} records the integrated verification result. The standalone
upper package is available through \texttt{import GaussianWave.BS}.

This upper stage is separate from the lower bound in Sections 6--10,
typical-process machinery, and the finite-graph BS qualitative theorem.
The spectral-endpoint one-zero-weight argument is also separate from the
strict-bulk target stated here. Channels share one limiting energy, as in a
compact bulk window. The earlier downstream eigenvector/universality package
still exposes its qualitative BS and quantitative one-edge inputs explicitly;
this upper theorem alone does not discharge those full source inputs.
